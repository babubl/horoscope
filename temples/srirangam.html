Srirangam Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple

Srirangam, on an island between the Kaveri and the Kollidam, is the first and foremost of the 108 Divya Desams of Lord Vishnu and one of the largest functioning temples in the world. Ranganatha reclines on the serpent Adisesha, facing south towards Lanka.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to tradition, the Sriranga Vimanam — the shrine with Ranganatha reclining upon it — arose from Brahma’s penance and remained for ages in Satyaloka, until King Ikshvaku brought it down to Ayodhya, where Rama’s ancestors worshipped it. After his victory, Rama gave it to Vibhishana to take to Lanka. On the way Vibhishana set it down on the island between the Kaveri and the Kollidam — and it would not move again. He left it in the care of a local king on one condition: that the Lord would always face south, towards Lanka. That is why Ranganatha reclines facing south.

Another story tells of a sultan’s daughter who fell in love with the Lord and followed his idol to Srirangam; she is worshipped here as Thulukka Nachiyar. And the saint Thiruppan Alvar, carried into the shrine on the shoulders of the sage Loka Saranga Muni, sang the ten verses of "Amalanadhipiran" before the Lord and is said to have merged with him.

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • The temple covers about 155 acres within seven concentric prakarams — a whole town lives inside its walls.
  • Its Rajagopuram, about 73 metres tall, was completed only in 1987.
  • The Paramapada Vasal (gate of Vaikuntha) opens once a year, on Vaikunta Ekadasi in Margazhi.
  • Thulukka Nachiyar is offered chapatis, and Ranganatha is dressed in a lungi during her festival.
  • Tradition holds that Ramanuja’s body is preserved, seated, in his shrine within the temple.

History and architecture

Eleven of the twelve Alvars sang of Srirangam in the Naalayira Divya Prabandham. Cholas, Pandyas, Hoysalas, the Vijayanagara kings and the Nayaks all added to it; it was raided in the 14th century and the deity was restored in 1371. The temple has seven concentric prakarams and 21 gopurams on about 155 acres; the Rajagopuram, completed in 1987, is about 73 metres tall. Ramanujacharya lived and taught here.

Parigaram done here

  • Prayers to Ranganatha and Ranganayaki for prosperity, peace and moksha.
  • Passing through the Paramapada Vasal (the gate of Vaikuntha) on Vaikunta Ekadasi is considered highly auspicious.
  • Saturday worship is popular, including for Saturn-related difficulties.

How to reach

About 10 km from Tiruchirappalli city; Srirangam has its own railway station and Tiruchi airport is about 15 km away. About 95 km from Kumbakonam.

Before you go

  • Traditional dress is recommended (veshti or dhoti for men, saree or churidar for women).
  • The complex is huge — allow at least two to three hours, more on Vaikunta Ekadasi.
