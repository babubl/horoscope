Keezhaperumpallam Naganathaswamy Temple

Keezhaperumpallam is the Navagraha temple of Ketu, a small village temple about 2 km from Poompuhar on the coast. Ketu is shown here with folded hands, worshipping Lord Shiva.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to the sthala puranam, when the devas and asuras churned the ocean of milk, the serpent Vasuki served as the rope. When the halahala poison rose and Shiva drank it to save the world, Vasuki — grieving that he had caused his Lord to swallow poison — fell here, in what was then a bamboo forest, and prayed for forgiveness. Shiva appeared to him as Naganathar and stayed on to bless devotees.

Another tradition tells of Swarbhanu, the asura who slipped among the devas to taste the nectar. Mohini struck off his head, but having tasted amrita he lived on: the head became Rahu, worshipped at Thirunageswaram, and the body, joined to a serpent’s head, became Ketu — worshipped here.

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • Ketu is shown as a five-hooded serpent form on a lion pedestal, hands folded in worship of Shiva.
  • The temple has two separate images of Surya — for the Sun’s northward (Uttarayanam) and southward (Dakshinayanam) journeys.
  • Prasadam here is eaten inside the temple and not taken home.
  • A Vasuki festival honouring the serpent of the churning legend is held in the Tamil month of Panguni.

History and architecture

The temple is believed to be Chola in origin, with the present structure dated by different sources to the late Chola or the Nayak period. The village appears in Appar’s Thevaram, making it a Thevara Vaippu sthalam.

Ketu has a separate west-facing black-granite shrine in the first prakaram, shown with a serpent hood, his hands joined in worship of Naganathar.

Parigaram done here

  • Red flowers and multi-coloured cloth for Ketu; horse-gram rice (kollu sadam) as naivedyam.
  • Lighting seven lamps; milk abhishekam on one’s birth-star day.
  • Special pujas during Rahu kalam and Yamagandam, and a homam on Rahu–Ketu peyarchi day.

How to reach

About 2 km from Poompuhar, 22–29 km from Mayiladuthurai and Sirkazhi, and about 62 km from Kumbakonam. Buses are infrequent except on peyarchi days; hire a cab or auto from Mayiladuthurai or Sirkazhi.

Before you go

  • Visit together with Thiruvenkadu (Mercury) and Poompuhar on the same day.
  • It is a small village temple — expect simple facilities.
