Suryanar Kovil

Suryanar Kovil is the Navagraha temple of the Sun, and the only one of the nine where the planet himself is the main deity. Every other graha has its own shrine here, so devotees say a visit to Suryanar Kovil is a visit to all nine.

History and architecture

An inscription in the temple dates it to the reign of Kulottunga Chola I (late 11th – early 12th century). In Chola records it is called Kulottungachola-Marttandalaya; Vijayanagara rulers, including Krishnadevaraya, added to it later.

Surya stands in the west-facing sanctum holding a lotus in each hand, with his two consorts beside him, and Guru’s shrine faces him directly. The temple is maintained by the Tamil Nadu HR&CE department.

The temple’s story

Tradition says the nine grahas cured the sage Kalava of leprosy. Brahma was angry that they had granted a boon directly to a human and cursed them with the same disease. They did penance in the white erukku forest here, and Lord Shiva at nearby Thirumangalakudi freed them and told them to bless devotees from this place.

As told in temple tradition.

Parigaram done here

  • Worship on Sundays, especially during the Sun’s dasa or bhukti.
  • Surya archana and Navagraha homam; sarkarai pongal is the traditional offering.
  • Some devotees offer a tulabharam of wheat or jaggery equal to their own weight.
  • The old practice of thala vasam: staying for 12 Sundays, bathing in the temple tanks and worshipping.

How to reach

About 15 km from Kumbakonam and 22 km from Mayiladuthurai. The nearest railway station is Aduthurai; Kumbakonam has more trains. Buses on the Kumbakonam–Mayiladuthurai route stop at Thirumangalakudi, a short walk away. Nearest airport: Tiruchi.

Before you go

  • By custom, devotees first worship at Prananatheswarar temple, Thirumangalakudi, then enter Suryanar Kovil: Vinayaka first, then Viswanathar–Visalakshi, Surya and Guru, and finally the other graha shrines.
  • Sundays and Ratha Saptami are crowded; arrive early.
