Thirukarukavur Garbharakshambigai Temple

At Thirukarukavur the goddess is Garbharakshambigai — "she who protects the womb". Couples hoping for a child, and expectant mothers, come from across South India to pray here and receive the blessed ghee or castor-oil prasadam.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to the sthala puranam, the sage Nidhruva and his wife Vedhikai lived a devout life by the Vennar river. One day, while the pregnant Vedhikai was resting, the sage Urdhvapada came to their door. She was slow to rise and serve him, and in anger he cursed her. When she was about to lose her unborn child, Vedhikai cried out to the goddess. Ambal appeared as Garbharakshambigai — "she who protects the womb" — and kept the child safe in a pot until it was born at full term, as the boy Naidhruvan.

It is also said that Kamadhenu came here and scratched the ground with her hooves, and the milk pond that formed is the temple tank, Ksheera Kundam. The land was once a forest of mullai (jasmine) creepers, and the Lord was found there as a swayambu lingam wrapped in the vines — so he is Mullaivananathar. The village name is read as karu (womb) + kaa (protect) + oor (village).

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • The lingam is said to be of anthill earth, so no water is poured on it; it is only coated with fragrant punugu.
  • Marks of jasmine creepers can be seen on the lingam, where the Lord was found entwined in the vines.
  • Unusually, all the other Navagrahas here face Surya.
  • The temple has 31 Chola-period inscriptions, from kings including Rajaraja I and Rajendra I.
  • It is the first of the five Pancha Aranya temples, traditionally visited at dawn before the other four.

History and architecture

A Paadal Petra sthalam on the south bank of the Kaveri, sung by Sambandar and Appar, with Chola inscriptions reported from the reigns of Parantaka I and Rajaraja I. It is the first of the Pancha Aranya kshetrams, the five "forest shrines" near Kumbakonam.

The lingam is said to be formed of hardened earth, so it is not bathed with water; only punugu (civet paste) is applied, and only through the temple.

Parigaram done here

  • Couples hoping for a child offer ghee to the goddess and take the blessed ghee home as prasadam, traditionally for 48 days.
  • Expectant mothers offer castor oil and take back the blessed oil for an easy delivery.
  • Families return after the birth to give thanks.
  • Prayer goes hand in hand with good medical care — never in place of it.

How to reach

About 8 km from Papanasam on the Thanjavur–Kumbakonam road; about 20 km from both Kumbakonam and Thanjavur. Nearest railway stations: Papanasam and Kumbakonam.

Before you go

  • Ghee and oil prasadam is given at the temple counter.
  • The Pancha Aranya circuit of five temples (about 36 km) traditionally starts here and is finished in one day.
