Rameswaram Ramanathaswamy Temple

Rameswaram is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four Char Dham pilgrimages. Families come here above all for pitru tarpanam — rites for departed ancestors — and for the bath in the 22 sacred wells inside the temple.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to tradition, after the war with Ravana, Rama wished to worship Shiva here to atone for the killing. He sent Hanuman north to Kailash to bring a lingam before the auspicious hour. Hanuman was delayed, so Sita shaped a lingam from the sand of the shore, and Rama worshipped it — the Ramalingam, the main deity today. When Hanuman returned and could not move Sita’s lingam, his own was installed beside it as the Vishwalingam, and Rama ordered that Hanuman’s lingam should always be worshipped first.

Many other stories cling to these waters: Sugreeva is said to have made the Sugreeva Theertham, and the sea at the eastern gate, where Agni bathed, is Agni Theertham. In the temple Vishnu is also worshipped as Sethumadhava, his feet in chains, from a story of how he won a Pandya princess. The Ramayana itself calls this shore supremely sacred, able to wash away the greatest sins.

Did you know?

  • Its corridors, nearly 1.2 km in all, are the longest of any Hindu temple in India; the outer corridor alone has about 1,212 pillars.
  • There are 22 theerthams — wells and tanks — inside the temple, each with its own name and taste.
  • It is the southernmost of India’s 12 Jyotirlingas and the only one in Tamil Nadu.
  • Hanuman’s Vishwalingam is still worshipped before the main Ramalingam, as Rama is said to have ordered.

History and architecture

The southernmost Jyotirlinga and a Paadal Petra sthalam sung by Appar, Sundarar and Sambandar. The Pandyas enlarged it in the 12th century and the Sethupathi rulers of Ramanathapuram built much of what stands today; Muthuramalinga Sethupathi completed the famous third corridor in the late 18th century. Its corridors are the longest of any Hindu temple, with about 1,200 pillars in the outer corridor.

Parigaram done here

  • Bathe in the sea at Agni Theertham, where many families perform pitru tarpanam on the shore.
  • Then bathe in the 22 theerthams (wells and tanks) inside the temple in their set order.
  • Change into dry clothes for darshan of Ramanathaswamy.
  • Rameswaram is also where the Kasi–Rameswaram pilgrimage begins and ends.

How to reach

Rameswaram has its own railway station; the island is linked to the mainland by the Pamban bridges. Ramanathapuram is about 60 km away and Madurai airport about 175 km.

Before you go

  • The theertham bath is ticketed and very crowded at dawn and on Amavasai.
  • Carry a spare set of clothes — darshan is only in dry clothes.
