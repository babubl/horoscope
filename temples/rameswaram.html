Rameswaram Ramanathaswamy Temple

Rameswaram is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four Char Dham pilgrimages. Families come here above all for pitru tarpanam — rites for departed ancestors — and for the bath in the 22 sacred wells inside the temple.

History and architecture

The southernmost Jyotirlinga and a Paadal Petra sthalam sung by Appar, Sundarar and Sambandar. The Pandyas enlarged it in the 12th century and the Sethupathi rulers of Ramanathapuram built much of what stands today; Muthuramalinga Sethupathi completed the famous third corridor in the late 18th century. Its corridors are the longest of any Hindu temple, with about 1,200 pillars in the outer corridor.

The temple’s story

Rama is said to have worshipped Shiva here after the war with Ravana. When Hanuman was delayed bringing a lingam from Kailash, Sita made one of sand — the Ramalingam. Rama then ordered that Hanuman’s lingam, the Vishwalingam, be worshipped first, which is still the custom.

As told in temple tradition.

Parigaram done here

  • Bathe in the sea at Agni Theertham, where many families perform pitru tarpanam on the shore.
  • Then bathe in the 22 theerthams (wells and tanks) inside the temple in their set order.
  • Change into dry clothes for darshan of Ramanathaswamy.
  • Rameswaram is also where the Kasi–Rameswaram pilgrimage begins and ends.

How to reach

Rameswaram has its own railway station; the island is linked to the mainland by the Pamban bridges. Ramanathapuram is about 60 km away and Madurai airport about 175 km.

Before you go

  • The theertham bath is ticketed and very crowded at dawn and on Amavasai.
  • Carry a spare set of clothes — darshan is only in dry clothes.
