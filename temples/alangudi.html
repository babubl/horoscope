Alangudi Apatsahayeswarar Temple

Alangudi is the Guru (Jupiter) sthalam of the Navagraha circuit. Guru is worshipped here as Dakshinamurthy, and the temple draws huge crowds on Thursdays and on Guru peyarchi day.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to the sthala puranam, this is where Shiva drank the deadly poison that rose when the devas and asuras churned the ocean of milk. The name Alangudi is linked to "aalam", that poison, and the Lord’s name Apatsahayeswarar means "the one who rescues in times of danger".

Parvati is said to have done penance here after Daksha’s sacrifice, so that she could be united with Shiva again. Another story tells how the saint Sundarar could not cross the flooded Vettaru river: Shiva came as a boatman and rowed him across, and when the boat struck a rock, Vinayaka saved it. That Vinayaka is still worshipped here as Kalangamal Katha Vinayakar — "he who kept us from distress".

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • An image of Sundarar here has pock marks on its face; legend says a priest once hid it from soldiers by claiming it was a child with chickenpox.
  • A goat-headed image of Daksha stands outside the main shrine, recalling the story of Daksha’s sacrifice.
  • Alangudi is one of the Pancha Aranya (five forest) temples, which pilgrims visit in a fixed order in a single day.
  • The temple is associated with sixteen sacred water bodies, including the Amirtha Pushkarini.

History and architecture

Thirugnana Sambandar’s hymn on this temple is in the First Thirumurai, making it a Paadal Petra sthalam; its old name was Thiru Irumpoolai. The temple is attributed to the Cholas, with the present structure largely from the Nayak period (16th century). It is one of the Pancha Aranya kshetrams.

Dakshinamurthy stands in the south-wall niche; unusually, his festival image is taken out in a chariot procession.

Parigaram done here

  • 24 ghee lamps and 24 rounds (pradakshinam) at the Guru shrine — the practice this temple is known for.
  • Thursday abhishekam and vastram (cloth) offerings to Dakshinamurthy.
  • Special worship on Masi Thursdays, when married women change the thread of their thali.
  • Devotees pray here for marriage, children and success in studies.

How to reach

About 17 km south of Kumbakonam on the road to Needamangalam and Mannargudi; the nearest railway station is Needamangalam (about 7 km). Buses from Kumbakonam and Tiruvarur. Nearest airport: Tiruchi.

Before you go

  • Thursdays and Guru peyarchi bring very long queues — come at opening time.
  • Traditional dress is expected, as at most Tamil Nadu temples.
