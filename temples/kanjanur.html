Kanjanur Agniswarar Temple

Kanjanur is the Navagraha temple of Venus (Sukran). Unusually, there is no separate Sukran idol to worship: by tradition Lord Shiva himself takes the place of Sukran here, so devotees pray to Agniswarar for Venus.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

According to the sthala puranam, Agni, the god of fire, kept for himself the offerings of Brahma’s sacrifice and was struck by a disease that even the Ashwini twins could not cure. On Brahma’s advice he worshipped Shiva in this palasa forest and called the waters of the Ganga, Yamuna and Saraswati into a tank. He was healed — and so the Lord is Agniswarar.

It is said that when Sukracharya, guru of the asuras, kept reviving fallen demons with his Mrita Sanjivini mantra, Shiva swallowed him, and Sukra remained in yogic absorption within Shiva. That is why Shiva himself is worshipped here as Sukra. Brahma is said to have witnessed the divine wedding of Shiva and Parvati here, and Nataraja revealed his Mukti Tandavam to the sage Parasara.

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • The Navagraha shrine is a recent addition — for centuries Shiva himself was worshipped here as Sukra.
  • Dakshinamurthy is shown teaching the local saint Haradatta Sivacharya, instead of in his usual pose with his foot on a demon.
  • A stone Nandi near the flagstaff is said to have eaten grass offered to it by Haradatta.
  • The Kaveri turns and flows northward at Kanjanur, which is considered especially sacred.

History and architecture

The main structure is Medieval Chola work, renovated under the Vijayanagara kings; inscriptions mention Chola kings including Vikrama Chola and Kulottunga Chola I. Appar praises the temple in the Thevaram.

The temple has a five-tier rajagopuram, two prakarams and the Agni and Parasara theerthams. Kanjanur is the birthplace of Manakkanjara Nayanar, one of the 63 Nayanmars, and is linked with the Shaiva saint Haradatta Sivacharya.

Parigaram done here

  • Friday worship for Sukran: white cloth, white flowers and field beans (mochai).
  • Prayers here for a happy married life and harmony between spouses (kalathra dosham).
  • Abhishekam to Agniswarar on Fridays during a Venus dasa or bhukti.

How to reach

On the Kumbakonam–Aduthurai road, about 18 km from Kumbakonam and only 2–3 km from Suryanar Kovil. Aduthurai is about 5 km away; Mayiladuthurai Junction is about 20 km. Buses from Kumbakonam and Mayiladuthurai.

Before you go

  • Visit it right after Suryanar Kovil — they are almost next to each other.
  • Fridays are busiest.
