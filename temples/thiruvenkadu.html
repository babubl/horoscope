Thiruvenkadu Swetharanyeswarar Temple

Thiruvenkadu is the Navagraha temple of Mercury (Budhan), where devotees pray for learning, speech and a good career. The large temple is also famous for Aghoramurthy, a fierce form of Shiva, and is called Adi Chidambaram.

History and architecture

The temple is a Paadal Petra sthalam praised in the 7th-century Thevaram. About a hundred stone inscriptions record gifts from Chola kings including Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, the Pandyas and Vijayanagara rulers such as Krishnadevaraya; much of the present stone structure is Vijayanagara work of the 16th century.

The complex covers about 12 acres and has three tanks — Surya, Chandra and Agni theertham. Budhan has his own shrine near the Chandra tank, beside the shrine of the goddess. Fine Chola bronzes from here are now in the Chennai Museum and the Thanjavur Art Gallery.

The temple’s story

Tradition says the demon Maruthuvasuran, made powerful by a boon, troubled the devas, who took refuge here. Shiva took the fierce form of Aghoramurthy and subdued him; the nine wounds from the demon’s trident are still pointed out on the temple Nandi.

As told in temple tradition.

Parigaram done here

  • Green gram (pachai payaru) offered to Budhan; green cloth for the deity.
  • Wednesday abhishekam and archana for Budhan, especially by students and those in business.
  • Aghoramurthy puja on Sunday nights, with special worship on the Sundays of Karthigai.

How to reach

On the Sirkazhi–Poompuhar road, about 12 km from Sirkazhi railway station, 27 km from Mayiladuthurai, 14 km from Vaitheeswaran Koil and about 62 km from Kumbakonam. Frequent buses from Sirkazhi and Mayiladuthurai.

Before you go

  • Use the western entrance to reach the Budhan shrine directly.
  • Keezhaperumpallam (Ketu) is on the same road towards Poompuhar; visit them together.
