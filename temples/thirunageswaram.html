Thirunageswaram Naganathaswamy Temple

Thirunageswaram is the Navagraha temple of Rahu, only 6 km from Kumbakonam. Rahu is worshipped here as "Mangala Rahu" with his two consorts, and devotees say the milk poured over him during the Rahu kalam abhishekam turns blue.

History and architecture

The temple is believed to have been built by Aditya Chola I in the 10th century and enlarged by later rulers; the ornate entrance hall is from the Nayak period. Sekkizhar, the author of the Periya Puranam, renovated it in the 12th century. It is a Paadal Petra sthalam sung by Sambandar and Appar.

Rahu has his own shrine in the second prakaram. Unlike most temples, he is shown in full human form, with his consorts Nagavalli and Nagakanni. The goddess Girigujambigai shares her shrine with Lakshmi and Saraswati.

The temple’s story

The serpent kings Adisesha, Dakshan and Karkotakan are said to have worshipped Shiva here, giving the place its name. Rahu himself is said to have been freed from a curse here by Shiva’s grace.

As told in temple tradition.

Parigaram done here

  • Milk abhishekam to Rahu during Rahu kalam, most popular on Sundays.
  • Rahu archana and homam during Rahu dasa or bhukti, and for Sarpa / Kala Sarpa dosham and delayed marriage.
  • Durga worship is closely linked with Rahu; many devotees also pray at the Durga shrine.

How to reach

About 6 km east of Kumbakonam on the road to Karaikal; frequent town buses. Kumbakonam is the nearest railway station. The Oppiliappan (Vishnu) temple is about 2 km away.

Before you go

  • Prasadam here is made without salt, and devotees are asked not to bring salty food into the temple.
  • Buy the abhishekam ticket early on Sundays; combine the visit with Oppiliappan temple.
