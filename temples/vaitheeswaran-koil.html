Vaitheeswaran Koil

Vaitheeswaran Koil is the Navagraha temple of Mars (Angarakan / Sevvai) and one of the most visited temples in Tamil Nadu. Shiva is worshipped here as Vaidyanathar, the divine healer, and Murugan as Selvamuthukumaraswamy.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

The old name of the town, Pullirukkuvelur, is said to remember those who worshipped here: "Pul" the bird Jatayu, "Irukku" the Rig Veda, "Vel" Lord Murugan, and "Ur" the Sun.

According to the sthala puranam, the devas begged Shiva for a cure for the 4,448 diseases of mankind. Following his word they found an anthill beneath a neem tree and poured Kamadhenu’s milk over it — and a self-manifested lingam appeared. Here Shiva is Vaidyanathar, the Divine Physician, and Parvati, who brought the healing oil (thailam) for his medicine, is Thaiyal Nayaki.

Angarakan (Mars) is said to have been cured of leprosy after bathing in the Siddhamirtham tank. Tradition also holds that Jatayu fell here after fighting Ravana, that Rama performed his last rites at Jatayu Kundam, and that Murugan received his Vel from Parvati at this place.

Did you know?

  • Unusually, the nine grahas stand in a single row behind the main shrine, all facing towards the Lord.
  • Devotees offer salt and pepper at the temple and dissolve jaggery in the Siddhamirtham tank, praying for relief from skin ailments.
  • An inscription records that Muthuswami Dikshitar composed kritis here on the goddess Thaiyal Nayaki.
  • Tradition says no frogs or snakes live in the Siddhamirtham tank because of a sage’s curse.
  • The town is famous for Nadi jothidam — readings from old palm-leaf manuscripts attributed to the sage Agathiyar.

History and architecture

The temple is a Paadal Petra sthalam sung by Thirugnana Sambandar and Appar in the 7th-century Thevaram, and Arunagirinathar sang of its Murugan in the Thiruppugazh. Its old name was Pullirukkuvelur. Inscriptions are mainly from the reign of Kulottunga Chola I; the Thanjavur Nayaks and Marathas were later patrons.

Angarakan has a separate shrine in the outer prakaram near the eastern gateway, and the other grahas stand in a row behind the sanctum. The temple is administered by the Dharmapuram Adheenam and is known for its Siddhamirtham tank.

Parigaram done here

  • Worship of Angarakan on Tuesdays and Krithigai days, with fasting.
  • Red cloth offered during the Tuesday abhishekam.
  • Salt, pepper and mustard placed at the Ambal shrine, and small silver figures offered, by those praying for healing.
  • Thiruchandu urundai (a pellet of sacred ash and soil) and the temple oil are taken home as prasadam.

How to reach

On the Mayiladuthurai–Chidambaram road: about 15 km from Mayiladuthurai, 7 km from Sirkazhi, 25 km from Chidambaram and about 55 km from Kumbakonam. The town has its own railway station; Mayiladuthurai and Sirkazhi have more trains.

Before you go

  • Tuesdays and Krithigai days are the most crowded.
  • The Nadi astrologers in the town are private businesses, not part of the temple.
  • Thiruvenkadu (Mercury) is about 14 km away; visit both on the same trip.
