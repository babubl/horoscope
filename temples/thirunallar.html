Thirunallar Saneeswaran Temple (Dharbaranyeswarar)

Thirunallar is the Navagraha temple of Saturn and the most visited of the nine. Saneeswara Bhagavan has his own shrine at the entrance of this ancient Shiva temple, and lakhs of devotees come on Sani peyarchi, when Saturn changes sign.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

The Lord here is Dharbaranyeswarar, "Lord of the forest of darbha grass" — the lingam is believed to have risen from darbha grass.

According to the sthala puranam, the devas were jealous when King Nala won Damayanti, and asked Saturn to test him. Nala lost his kingdom, his wealth and even his wife. At last he reached this shrine and bathed in the tank now called Nala Theertham. Saturn, it is said, dared not follow him into Shiva’s presence; he released Nala and stayed at the threshold — and, as Nala asked, he still guards the entrance and eases the suffering of all who come.

Another story tells of a shepherd who supplied milk for the abhishekam while an accountant stole it. When the innocent shepherd was about to be punished, Shiva sent his trident against the thief — and Nandi stepped aside to let it pass.

As told in temple tradition.

Did you know?

  • The Nandi and bali peetham stand slightly off the temple’s axis — explained by the story of the shepherd and the trident.
  • Saneeswaran is taken out in procession on a golden crow vahanam on festival days.
  • Thirunallar is one of the seven Saptha Vidanga temples; its Thyagaraja dances the Unmatta Natanam.
  • Tradition says a palm leaf of Sambandar’s hymn on this temple came out of fire unburnt and green during his debate in Madurai — so it is called the "Pachai Padhigam" (green hymn).

History and architecture

The present stone temple goes back to the Chola period, with later Vijayanagara additions. It is a Paadal Petra sthalam sung by Thirugnana Sambandar and Appar, and one of the seven Saptha Vidanga sthalams of Thyagaraja worship. Tradition says it houses an emerald (maragatha) lingam brought by the king Muchukunda.

Saneeswaran stands at the threshold of the main temple and is worshipped first; he is the only graha given the title "Eswaran".

Parigaram done here

  • Bathe in Nala Theertham (traditionally after applying sesame oil) before darshan.
  • Light a sesame-oil lamp and offer karunkuvalai (blue water-lily) flowers to Saneeswaran.
  • Many devotees wear black and feed crows with sesame rice in the prakaram.
  • Recite or listen to Sambandar’s padhigam on this temple, which the temple itself recommends.

How to reach

About 5 km west of Karaikal town and railway station, 22 km from Nagapattinam, 35 km from Mayiladuthurai and about 53 km from Kumbakonam. Thirunallar is in the Union Territory of Puducherry, not Tamil Nadu.

Before you go

  • Saturdays and Sani peyarchi days are extremely crowded; a weekday morning is calmest.
  • Order of worship: bath in Nala Theertham, then Saneeswaran at the entrance, then Dharbaranyeswarar and the goddess.
