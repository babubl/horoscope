Thanjavur Big Temple (Brihadeeswarar / Peruvudaiyar Kovil)

The Big Temple of Thanjavur, built by Rajaraja Chola I and consecrated around 1010 CE, is one of the greatest temples in India and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its granite vimana rises about 60 metres over the sanctum of Lord Shiva as Peruvudaiyar.

Sthala varalaru — the temple’s story

The temple is known more for its recorded royal history than for a puranic legend. Tradition says Rajaraja built it after a divine command to raise a shrine to Shiva on a scale never seen before.

As told in temple tradition.

History and architecture

Rajaraja Chola I built the temple, originally named Rajarajesvaram, and consecrated it in his 25th regnal year, around 1009–1010 CE. The 13-storey vimana is about 60 metres tall, and a huge monolithic Nandi faces the sanctum. The walls carry hundreds of inscriptions recording the king’s gifts, the temple staff and its dancers and musicians.

UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1987; in 2004 the listing was extended as the "Great Living Chola Temples" to include Gangaikondacholapuram and Darasuram.

Parigaram done here

  • Abhishekam to Peruvudaiyar and the great Nandi, especially on Pradosham evenings.
  • Many families visit on Sadhaya Vizha and on Maha Shivaratri.
  • Thanjavur is the natural starting point for Thingalur (Moon) and Thirukarukavur.

How to reach

In Thanjavur city, about 2 km from Thanjavur Junction railway station and about 40 km from Kumbakonam. Nearest airport: Tiruchi, about 55 km.

Before you go

  • Wear modest clothes and leave footwear at the counter.
  • Early morning and evening light are best for seeing the vimana; carry water in summer — the stone courtyard gets very hot.
