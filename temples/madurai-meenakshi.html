Madurai Meenakshi Amman Temple

The Meenakshi Amman temple is the heart of Madurai. Goddess Meenakshi, who by tradition ruled Madurai as a Pandya queen before marrying Lord Shiva as Sundareswarar, is worshipped first here, and her wedding is re-enacted every Chithirai before lakhs of devotees.

History and architecture

A Paadal Petra sthalam praised in the Thevaram. The oldest surviving parts date to the Pandya king Jatavarman Kulasekara I (around 1190–1216). The temple was looted in 1311 and rebuilt after Vijayanagara took Madurai; the Madurai Nayaks — especially Vishwanatha Nayak and Thirumalai Nayak — gave it its present form in the 16th–17th centuries, including the Thousand-Pillar Hall. It has 14 gopurams; the southern tower is about 52 metres tall.

The temple’s story

King Malayadhwaja Pandya and Queen Kanchanamalai received a little girl from a sacrificial fire. She grew up to rule Madurai, conquered the world, and on meeting Shiva at Kailash recognised him as her husband; their wedding took place in Madurai and is celebrated every year.

As told in temple tradition.

Parigaram done here

  • Worship Meenakshi first, then Sundareswarar.
  • Families pray here for marriage and a harmonious family life; witnessing the Thirukalyanam is considered especially auspicious.
  • Many devotees walk around the Golden Lotus tank (Porthamarai Kulam) before darshan.

How to reach

In central Madurai, a short ride from Madurai Junction railway station; Madurai airport is about 12 km away.

Before you go

  • Phones and cameras are usually deposited at the entrance counters.
  • Fridays and festival days have long queues; early morning is calmest.
